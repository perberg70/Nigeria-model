What these ranges mean, and how the maps are built

Straight from the World Bank's CCKP CMIP6 ensemble at 0.25°. The national figure is the ensemble median for the country. A region is the median of its departments: each climate model is first summarised across the departments in a region, and the median and 10th–90th percentiles are then taken across those model results. A region is an unweighted median, so a large, thinly populated department counts as much as a small, dense one.

The maps draw Colombia's 32 mainland departments from geoBoundaries (OpenStreetMap, ODbL 1.0), shaded by the natural region each belongs to; thick lines are region borders. San Andrés y Providencia sits in the dashed box, off its true position.

Source: World Bank Climate Change Knowledge Portal, CMIP6 collection at 0.25°, retrieved 24 August 2026.

This is one possible result — what could make it higher or lower

The rainfall range spans zero for Colombia and for every region on this pathway. The models disagree on whether it gets wetter or drier, so a median is the middle of a disagreement rather than a consensus, and a rainfall figure quoted without its range overstates what is known. SSP1-1.9 has no regional model range, because CCKP publishes no individual model runs for that pathway.
