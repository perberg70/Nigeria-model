5. BACK-UP GENERATORS: THE BOUNDARY TERM
  The prototype counts an estimated 20.6 TWh of genset output as electricity, while
  the fuel stays in final energy if it is recorded at all (UNSD books all petrol and
  almost all diesel to road transport; the IEA says genset inputs and outputs "may not be
  properly reported", p. 502). Replacing that output with grid power at constant service:
    genset efficiency 15%: fuel 494 PJ, final energy falls by 420 PJ (17.1% of 2023)
    genset efficiency 20%: fuel 371 PJ, final energy falls by 297 PJ (12.1% of 2023)
    genset efficiency 25%: fuel 297 PJ, final energy falls by 222 PJ (9.1% of 2023)
  ...if the fuel is inside final energy. If it is not recorded, final energy is
  understated today and nothing falls. The prototype's 2023 electrification share
  (8.99%, older release) also uses gross generation plus genset output, against 4.88%
  final electricity in the balance; the share definition is itself a boundary choice.

7. WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE DEMAND LAW
  eps_final = eps_primary - eps_P/F. The paper estimates eps_primary on 1960-2010 data,
  when electrification was thermal and raised P/F with income. Its own sector columns
  imply eps_P/F of about +0.07 at Nigeria's percentile and +0.12 to +0.19 in the long
  run (CONVERSION_FACTORS.md section C: final 0.28-0.30 vs total 0.36; 0.52-0.59 vs 0.71).
  IMAGE R10, 2020 -> 2050: eps_P/F = ssp119 +0.043, ssp126 -0.005, ssp245 +0.017, ssp585 +0.102
  IMAGE R10, 2020 -> 2100: eps_P/F = ssp119 +0.036, ssp126 +0.033, ssp245 +0.053, ssp585 +0.077
